Founded by 15-year-old Festus Bradley Nyadimo in Kisumu, Kenya, the company ships its BradVector software toolchain (assembler, interpreter, debugger, profiler) before its silicon is fabbed, so every claim carries a receipt. Its ecosystem spans BradOS, BradCore, HyperCore and BradXon processors, Torox GPUs, and the BradSense-Render neural rendering engine.

The story

Festus Bradley Nyadimo was born in Kisumu, Kenya. At 15, he is designing a complete computer ecosystem — the silicon, the memory, the operating system and the software that builds them — because he believes the next great technology company can be built from Africa.

Brad Devices is not a slide deck. The software toolchain runs today: you can open the website and watch it assemble, execute and debug BradVector code in your browser. The silicon is designed to the same standard, documented in a 1,737-line unified specification, and tagged honestly — SHIPPED, DESIGNED, or VISION — so a reader always knows exactly what a file can prove.

For the founder's own account of the eleven months between his first ten essays and the first release, see The Receipts, One Year Later — published 7 October 2026. It is the shortest honest account of what this company is and is not, and it is quotable without contacting anyone.

The company's philosophy is "receipts before promises": every claim carries a tag and a link to verify it. No invented benchmarks. No pretend silicon. The receipts are the product.

The two rules

Two ISAs, never one

BradISA is the CPU. BradVector is the GPU. They meet on the BradFusion fabric and share SPMP memory — they do not share instructions.

Receipts before promises

Every claim carries a tag — SHIPPED (software runs today), DESIGNED (silicon designed), VISION (honest future) — and a link to verify it.
